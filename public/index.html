Most teams don't need more process. They need clear priorities, honest feedback, and the context and tooling to do their best work. My job is to make those easier to get.

I still sit in design and code reviews. Leaders who understand the details can defend their teams' decisions and spot trouble early.

Selected work

Tax and fee processing system

Problem: Every deal has to get tax, title, and fees right, and the rules change state by state. Wrong amounts mean rework, audit exposure, and a bad moment for the customer at the exact time they decided to buy.

What I did: Developed fundamental components of CarMax's tax and fee processing system. Designed and strengthened systems and data integrations for tax and fee processes. Analyzed and implemented complex business rules for point-of-sale processes.

Result: A dependable core that every store deal runs through, with business rules maintainable enough for later teams to extend them without guessing.

Learn more

Tax and fee correctness is the worst place for a surprise: it sits between regulatory compliance, accounting, and the customer's moment of purchase. This work was about treating rule complexity as a design problem — separating the rules from the code that runs them so changes across states and years stayed manageable.

PCI financial integration

Problem: Payments handled under PCI rules require an auditable, compliant path end to end — and it can't slow the store down or interrupt the revenue that flows through it every day.

What I did: Created and implemented a critical financial integration necessary for PCI compliance.

Result: Stores ran their financial processes inside a compliant integration, without adding friction at the counter.

Learn more

The hard part of a compliance-driven integration is that all the constraints arrive at once: security requirements from PCI, throughput demands from the stores, and coexistence with legacy systems that can't simply stop. The integration had to be secure by design rather than retrofitted afterward.

Enterprise-wide security system

Problem: As customer data became a bigger target, security checks varied team to team, and personally identifiable information had accumulated in places nobody had systematically looked.

What I did: Designed and supervised development of an essential enterprise-wide security system. Identified and remediated organizational PII data security risks.

Result: A security system the company could build on, plus an organization-wide review and remediation of PII risk rather than a team-by-team patchwork.

Learn more

Designing the system was half the work; supervising development of it across teams was the other half. The PII work meant looking everywhere customer data could live, rating the exposure honestly, and fixing it — a breadth exercise as much as a technical one.

Salesforce and Azure cloud transformation

Problem: Store application delivery was tied to a legacy development model. Moving to Salesforce and Azure meant re-platforming and re-skilling a team mid-stream, without stalling the delivery the business depended on.

What I did: Led team through technical transformation into Salesforce and Azure cloud development. Provided technical design and implementation oversight to several product delivery teams. Facilitated design and code reviews spanning a variety of software technologies.

Result: Teams shipping on the new platforms with quality standards held up during the shift — the team leveled up while delivery kept going.

Learn more

The transformation worked because it was carried by the design and code reviews, not around them: standards traveled through the reviews that spanned both platforms and a mix of technologies, which kept quality up even as the tooling underneath the team changed.
